Buying land is exciting. Whether it's a lot near Canyon Lake or acreage outside the city, it's a chance to build exactly what you want. But land has questions a finished house doesn't, and the answers can change what the property is really worth to you.

## Access and easements

Make sure the property has legal access from a public road, not just a path that's been used for years. Look for easements that let others cross the land, like utility lines or a neighbor's driveway, and find out how they affect where you can build.

## Utilities: water, power, and septic

In many Hill Country areas, there's no city water or sewer.

- **Water:** is there a water company or co-op available, or will you need a well? Ask about typical well depths and costs nearby.
- **Septic:** most rural lots need an on-site sewage system, which is permitted and must suit the soil. Some soils need more expensive systems.
- **Power:** find out how far the nearest line is. Extending service a long way can be costly.

## Restrictions and rules

- **Deed restrictions or HOA rules** can limit home size, building materials, fencing, livestock, or short-term rentals.
- **County and city rules** cover permits, setbacks, and in some areas additional environmental requirements. Parts of the San Antonio region sit over the Edwards Aquifer, where extra rules can apply to development.

## Flood zones and the lay of the land

Check flood maps and look at the land after a heavy rain if you can. Slope, rock, and drainage all affect where a house can sit and what site work will cost.

## Survey, minerals, and taxes

- Get a current **survey** so you know exactly where the boundaries are.
- In Texas, **mineral rights** are sometimes owned separately from the surface. Ask whether they convey with the land.
- If the land has an **agricultural or open-space valuation**, learn what's required to keep it and what happens if the use changes, since rollback taxes can apply.
