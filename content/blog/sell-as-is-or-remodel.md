One of the most common questions sellers ask is whether to fix things up before listing. There's no single right answer. It depends on your home's condition, your timeline, your budget, and what buyers in your price range expect.

## When selling as-is makes sense

- You need to move quickly, or you can't be on site to manage work.
- The home needs major repairs that would cost more than they add in value.
- The likely buyer is an investor or someone who plans to renovate anyway.
- Cash for upfront repairs isn't available, or you'd rather not take on the risk.

Selling as-is doesn't mean hiding problems. Texas sellers generally still need to disclose known issues, and buyers will still have the home inspected. It simply means the price reflects the condition.

## When updating first pays off

Some projects tend to return more than they cost, especially when a home is close to "move-in ready":

- **Fresh paint** in neutral colors.
- **Small repairs** a buyer's inspector would flag: leaks, broken fixtures, damaged trim.
- **Lighting and hardware** that make dated rooms feel current.
- **Curb appeal:** trimming, mulch, a clean entry, and a front door that looks cared for.
- **Deep cleaning** and clearing clutter, which costs little and changes how a home shows.

Large projects, like a full kitchen remodel or a new addition, rarely recover their full cost at sale. They can also delay your listing by months.

## Think like the buyer

Ask what a buyer at your price point is comparing your home to. If similar homes nearby are updated, an older kitchen may stand out. If they aren't, the same kitchen may be perfectly normal for the neighborhood.
